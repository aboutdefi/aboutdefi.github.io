Avalanche swap slippage: what beginners need to know

Slippage tolerance sets the minimum output an Avalanche swap will accept; beginners can use it to weigh failed trades against receiving fewer tokens.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 4, 20263 min read e33b10

On an Avalanche decentralized exchange, slippage tolerance sets how much the swap’s final output may fall below its quoted amount before the trade reverts. It gives the transaction room for price changes while it waits to execute, but a higher setting can let you receive fewer tokens.

What does slippage tolerance change?

LFJ’s documentation defines slippage as the difference between expected and actual output, which can change with volatility or limited liquidity. The tolerance sets the boundary: if the executed amount falls below the minimum output, the swap cancels. The minimum is calculated from the quote and your chosen percentage.

For example, if a quote shows 100 tokens and tolerance is 1%, the minimum output is 99 tokens. The trade can complete at 99 or more; below that, it reverts. A failed swap may still cost network fees, LFJ says, so a very tight setting can mean paying for a transaction that does not complete.

How is slippage different from price impact?

Price impact is the effect your trade has on the pool’s price; slippage is the difference between the expected and executed output. Pangolin’s Avalanche swap guide says larger trades and pools with less liquidity can produce greater price impact. That cost may already appear in the initial quote, while slippage tolerance covers further changes before execution.

Gas settings control the transaction’s network fee and processing priority; slippage controls the output the swap will accept. For the separate question of Blackhole swap gas settings for speed and cost, see the dedicated guide. Changing gas settings does not raise the minimum output or make a poor quote better.

What setting should a beginner use?

Start with the exchange’s suggested or automatic tolerance, then check the minimum output before confirming. There is no universal percentage that suits every token and market: a volatile asset or thin pool may need more room than a liquid, steady pair. Raising tolerance can help a swap execute, but it also permits a worse result.

  • Check the token pair, amount, quoted output and minimum output.
  • Compare price impact with your intended trade size.
  • If the required tolerance looks unusually high, consider a smaller amount or wait for conditions to change.
  • Keep AVAX available for network fees; Avalanche Support says C-Chain transaction fees are paid in AVAX.

A tolerance setting is a limit, not a price guarantee: the trade can execute anywhere between the quote and the stated minimum. Review both figures before signing; if the minimum is unacceptable, do not submit the swap.
