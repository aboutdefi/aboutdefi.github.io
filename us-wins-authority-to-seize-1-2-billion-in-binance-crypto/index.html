US wins authority to seize $1.2 billion in Binance crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 18, 2026 · 12:38 UTC2 min read 4dd46b

The U.S. Justice Department said Nov. 12 it had obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, after a theft of more than $6 billion on or about Oct. 29. The department’s announcement says the funds are part of a broader effort to stop North Korean actors from laundering the stolen assets.

The figures mark different stages of the recovery effort. The Justice Department said more than $2.7 billion had been frozen through U.S. government and private-sector work with virtual asset service providers; the court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that total.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, took the virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: The combined U.S. government and private-sector response has immobilized this amount of the stolen funds so far.
  • More than $1.2 billion under court authority: The department can seize or restrain this portion of the frozen assets. That authorization does not mean all of it has been forfeited.

The FBI had attributed the theft to North Korea in an Oct. 31 alert, saying the actors were converting the assets primarily into ether and urging service providers to block transactions tied to listed addresses. The Justice Department says the actors continue to move assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges as authorities trace and pursue them.

The Associated Press reported the FBI described the amount as seized. It also noted that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. The next step is continued recovery work as the stolen cryptocurrency is moved through exchanges and other services.
