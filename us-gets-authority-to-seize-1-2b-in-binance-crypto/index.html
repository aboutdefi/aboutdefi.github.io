US gets authority to seize $1.2B in Binance crypto

The Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, as efforts to trace and recover the funds continue.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 19, 2026 · 08:19 UTC2 min read 096981

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, Nov. 12. The funds are part of a theft the FBI attributes to North Korean hackers; the department says recovery efforts are continuing. The Justice Department’s announcement sets out the seizure authority and the wider recovery effort.

The theft took place on or about Oct. 29, when North Korean military members stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance, according to the department. The FBI identifies the actors as TraderTraitor. The Associated Press reported the estimated theft at about $6.3 billion.

The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of a larger amount frozen since the theft. The Justice Department says U.S. authorities and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total, with more than $1.2 billion of that subject to court-authorized seizure or restraint. The Associated Press report notes that a forfeiture claim would typically go before a judge, who would determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The distinction matters: the $1.2 billion is not the full amount stolen, and the government’s court authority does not mean every asset has already been transferred or returned to victims. The department said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, while investigators and industry partners work to trace and block them.

The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance funds, the Justice Department said. Tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain ongoing; any return of funds will depend on the forfeiture process and claims to the assets.
