How to follow tokens after you click swap on SyncSwap

dek: What happens when I click swap on SyncSwap? The router sends input through a pool; you receive output if it meets your signed minimum in one transaction.

SyncSwap is a decentralized exchange that routes token trades through liquidity pools and returns the output to your wallet. When you click swap, your wallet signs a call that specifies the input amount, token path and minimum output you will accept.

For an ERC-20 input, an approval gives the router permission to move the approved tokens. That allowance is separate from the swap call; the swap itself carries the amount and minimum output you sign. The SyncSwap protocol routes trades through liquidity pools, so the pool’s calculation determines how much comes back.

Once the swap call executes, the router checks the route and pulls the input into the Vault. The route connects the input token to a pool that can trade it for the requested output token.

The router sends input through the Vault to a pool

The Vault holds the tokens as the swap is processed, while the pool calculates output using its curve and the trade’s input. Classic Pool and Stable Pool are pool types with different pool designs; the selected pool’s curve determines the trade’s output.

Pool Master registers pool factories and fee settings. The applicable fee is accounted for in the trade before the output is transferred to your wallet. The exact output depends on the pool calculation and fee for that route.

Your signed minimum is the threshold that protects the trade from settling below the amount you accepted. It is fixed in the signed call. If the pool produces more than that minimum when the transaction executes, you receive the full actual output, not just the minimum.

Your minimum decides whether the swap settles

If the calculated output meets or exceeds your minimum, the router completes the route and sends the output tokens to your wallet. The swap’s steps run as one atomic on-chain transaction: they succeed together or revert together.

If output falls below your minimum, the transaction reverts, so the input tokens are not left swapped or sent on to you as a partial trade. The failed transaction still uses gas. That distinction matters: your minimum sets the acceptable result, while the actual output can be higher if execution allows.

So clicking swap starts a signed route from wallet to Vault to pool and back to wallet. Because those swap steps are atomic, you either receive the route’s full output at or above your minimum, or the trade reverts and your tokens stay put.
