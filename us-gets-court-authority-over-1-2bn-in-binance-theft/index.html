US gets court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department won court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft proceeds as US and private partners continue tracking the stolen crypto.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 15, 2026 · 11:22 UTC2 min read 6ee9bc

US authorities obtained court authorization on Nov. 12 to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said in its announcement of the action. The FBI identified the theft as the work of North Korean military hackers it calls TraderTraitor.

The theft took place on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets, according to the Justice Department. The agency said US government and private sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion since the theft; the court-authorized amount is part of that frozen total.

  • More than $6 billion stolen: the total the Justice Department attributes to the Binance theft.
  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: assets interdicted through combined US government and private sector efforts.
  • More than $1.2 billion subject to court authority: the amount the department may seize or restrain from the frozen funds.

The figures describe different stages of the recovery effort. The $2.7 billion is frozen, while the court authorization allows the government to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of it; it does not mean that amount has been returned to Binance or its users. The Associated Press reported that recovered funds in such cases typically go through a forfeiture claim, with a judge deciding who has a legitimate claim.

The Justice Department said tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work is continuing. It said TraderTraitor actors are laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that the FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions linked to addresses used in that laundering.
