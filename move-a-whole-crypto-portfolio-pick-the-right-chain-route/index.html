Move a Whole Crypto Portfolio: Pick the Right Chain Route

Moving a whole crypto portfolio to another chain? Convert to USDC and bridge once when holdings lack native deployments; bridge high-gain native tokens directly.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 28, 2026 · 04:51 UTC3 min read 06bd3d

One native USDC bridge transfer is usually cheaper for a portfolio with small positions or tokens that lack native deployments on the destination.

Which route fits your holdings?

Usually, consolidate those tokens into USDC, bridge once, then rebuy. Bridge a token directly when it has an issuer-native deployment on the destination and selling it would realize large gains.

  1. Start with your source-chain portfolio. Gather the destination wallet address, gas on both chains, and each token’s cost basis. List every holding, then confirm whether its issuer deploys it natively on the destination. Without a native deployment, a bridge may deliver a wrapped version, or the token may have no route.
  2. Price both paths before swapping. Estimate consolidation as the slippage and gas for each sale, plus one USDC bridge fee and the slippage and gas for destination buys. Estimate direct bridging as the bridge fee and destination gas for each token transfer. Pool depth, transfer count and destination gas drive the totals.
  3. Include the tax cost of selling. Converting a token to USDC is a crypto-to-crypto disposal in jurisdictions that tax those trades. The IRS treats exchanging one crypto asset for another as taxable in the US. Compare each sale’s proceeds with its cost basis: a holding with a large unrealized gain can make direct bridging cheaper overall, even when it adds transfer fees. Tax rules vary by jurisdiction; this is not tax advice.
  4. Swap selected tokens to USDC. Sell the non-native or small positions on the source chain. Split swaps into tranches when a pool is thin to manage slippage. At this step, use the USDC bridge to move the consolidated balance in one transfer.
  5. Bridge the tokens you kept. Use a route that delivers the issuer’s native token on the destination. Circle CCTP burns USDC on the source chain and mints native USDC on the destination, including for supported routes such as Ethereum to Arbitrum, Base or Solana. Confirm chain support and token contracts before signing; a generic bridge can deliver wrapped assets.
  6. Rebuy and verify on the destination. Use the bridged USDC to buy the planned tokens, then inspect each balance’s contract address against the issuer’s native contract. Keep the token map, cost bases and route estimates together; reusing them and splitting thin-pool swaps saves time and helps manage slippage on the next migration.

What about tax records or tokens with no route?

How do I record a bridge transfer for tax purposes?

Keep transaction hashes, timestamps, source and destination chains, wallet addresses, token amounts, fees, and cost-basis records. Record the USDC swaps separately from the bridge transfer, and check local rules or ask a tax professional how to report them.

What if a token has no bridge route?

Check whether the issuer supports a native deployment on the destination. If not, compare selling it for USDC and rebuying an available asset with keeping it on the source chain; do not send it through an unverified route.
