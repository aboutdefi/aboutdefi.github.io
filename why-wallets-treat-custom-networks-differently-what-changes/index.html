Why wallets treat custom networks differently: What changes

Why does my wallet treat custom networks differently from built-in ones? Built-in networks include wallet-chosen details; custom ones need your checks.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 26, 2026 · 11:56 UTC2 min read bf2542

Built-in networks come with details selected and maintained by the wallet, while a custom network contains only the settings you or a site supplied. Your wallet can offer fewer automatic features and show extra warnings because it cannot vouch for that network’s RPC endpoint, symbol or block explorer.

Adding a network is a handoff of settings, not a wallet endorsement. A site can request it through EIP-3085’s wallet_addEthereumChain, or you can enter the details yourself.

Those details usually include a network name, RPC endpoint, Chain ID, native currency and, optionally, a block explorer. For a practical check, compare the proposed Chain ID and RPC endpoint with the network’s own information and Chainlist before saving; the directory gives you a reference for EVM network details, not a guarantee that an endpoint is safe.

A custom network asks you to verify the connection details

A Chain ID identifies a network for transaction signing under EIP-155. MetaMask also queries eth_chainId on the added RPC endpoint, then compares the reported chain ID and symbol with its reference data from chainid.network.

Say a site asks you to add a network and the Chain ID you enter differs from the one returned by its RPC endpoint. MetaMask may warn that the details do not match. A mismatch can mean a typo, stale settings or a misleading request, so stop and compare the values against the network’s own published information before approving.

MetaMask’s support guidance says it performs basic validation and warns when information seems wrong or unusual, but does not verify custom networks. A matching entry is a useful cross-check, not proof that the RPC endpoint or site is trustworthy.

Prices and token detection may need extra work

With a custom network, token balances can appear even when the wallet has no price data for those assets. Automatic token detection may also be limited, so a token might not show until you add its contract details yourself.

In practice, use a built-in network when it meets your needs; when adding a custom one, check the Chain ID, RPC endpoint, native currency symbol and block explorer before connecting or transacting. That quick comparison catches wrong settings early and helps explain missing prices or tokens later.
