DOJ seeks forfeiture of $1.1B in USDC tied to Binance hack

The Justice Department filed a civil forfeiture claim for more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to Binance’s October hack, while about $2.35 billion remains in linked wallets.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 25, 2026 · 05:11 UTC2 min read 5460fd

The U.S. Justice Department filed a civil claim on Nov. 23 to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC traced to the Oct. 29 Binance hack, according to the department’s announcement. The claim seeks legal ownership of funds already under a government-requested restraining order; it does not mean the court has granted forfeiture.

Circle would issue replacement USDC if forfeiture is ordered

The complaint identifies the funds as USDC issued by Circle Internet Financial and held across multiple blockchain addresses. It says Circle cannot transfer the frozen tokens from addresses controlled by the alleged hackers, but has agreed to issue an equivalent amount of new USDC and transfer it to the United States if the court orders forfeiture.

The filing says Binance reported an unauthorized network intrusion and theft of about $6 billion in cryptocurrency. The FBI, U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation assess that North Korean military hacking groups known as Lazarus Group and APT38 carried out the theft, according to the forfeiture complaint.

Authorities report more than $2.9 billion frozen, seized or returned

The Justice Department said U.S. authorities have helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion in cryptocurrency from multiple sources since the theft. That total is part of the broader recovery effort and is separate from the USDC targeted in this filing. The department said the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are investigating.

Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic estimated that about $2.35 billion remained in wallets linked to the theft, CoinDesk reported. Elliptic also said at least $247 million had passed through coinjoin or mixer transactions, which combine users’ funds to make transfers harder to trace.

The court must decide the forfeiture claim

The case now proceeds through civil forfeiture, with the court to decide whether the government can take ownership of the claimed assets. If the court grants the order, Circle has said it will issue the replacement USDC described in the complaint.
