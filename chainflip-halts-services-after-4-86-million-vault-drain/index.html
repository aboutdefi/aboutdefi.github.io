Chainflip halts services after $4.86 million vault drain

Chainflip paused Ethereum vault operations after a $4.86 million outflow, sending FLIP down more than 30% as investigators assess a valid but unusual signature.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 22, 2026 · 05:17 UTC2 min read 4b7a14

Chainflip paused deposits, withdrawals, swaps and quotes on Nov. 21 after about $4.86 million in ether and stablecoins left its Ethereum vault, and FLIP fell more than 30%, according to CoinDesk’s report.

Chainflip said it was investigating a potential exploit and had put the protocol into full safe mode. Blockchain trackers reported that one transaction moved 520.22 ETH, about 3.25 million USDC and roughly 758,000 USDT from the vault. Chainflip has not confirmed the loss amount or its cause.

How did the vault funds leave?

The withdrawal carried a valid signature from the key validators use to approve vault payments, CoinDesk reported, citing on-chain analysis. That points to a possible key compromise, but does not establish how the transaction was authorized.

Blockchain analytics firm Bitquery found that the transaction’s signature used a number outside Chainflip’s usual running count. Bitquery said the vault had accepted 299,677 signatures since 2023, and the drain was the only one that broke that count. Its analysis found the funds were converted to ether and deposited into Tornado Cash within minutes.

Who is affected by the pause?

Chainflip users cannot make swaps or withdraw liquidity while the protocol remains in safe mode. CoinDesk reported that the pause also stops payouts on swaps already in progress; deposits that had not been finalized will be processed after Chainflip exits safe mode.

The protocol held about $13.3 million in total value locked, according to DeFiLlama data cited by CoinDesk. The reported outflow came from its Ethereum vault. Chainflip has not said whether it will reimburse users affected by this incident.

What happens next?

Chainflip is investigating and has not announced a timetable for restoring service. Bitquery’s analysis found that validators switched to a new key less than two hours after the drain, but said the on-chain record does not show whether a key leaked or a security check failed. The protocol’s findings and any decision on user reimbursement remain outstanding.
