Which bridge providers does LI.FI use? Route examples

Which bridge providers does LI.FI use? LI.FI can route through Across, Stargate, Mayan, Circle CCTP or Relay; the provider varies by chain, tokens and route.

LI.FI can route transfers through providers such as Across, Stargate, Mayan, Circle CCTP and Relay; the provider depends on the chains, tokens and route selected.

  • LI.FI compares routes across supported providers.
  • Available providers vary with the transfer.
  • Route steps show which provider handles each part.

A route can involve more than one service, so the provider names matter at the step level. The lifi bridge as an aggregator is a concrete example: route aggregation brings options together, while the steps show which provider handles each leg. That is the practical distinction behind a lifi bridge.

LI.FI offers routes across multiple providers

LI.FI’s available routes depend on the source and destination chains and the tokens selected. Its cross-chain sources include Across, Stargate, Mayan and Relay, and it also integrates Circle CCTP. These are examples of providers LI.FI can use, not a fixed list that applies to every transfer.

Say you want to move one token from chain X to chain Y and receive a different token. LI.FI compares supported routes for that chain and token pair. A route may combine a bridge provider for the cross-chain transfer with a decentralized exchange provider for a token swap.

Route steps identify the provider for each leg

To see which provider a LI.FI route uses, compare the options for your transfer, then open a route’s steps. Read each step’s provider name to see which service handles the bridge and which handles any swap.

  1. Select the source chain, destination chain and tokens.
  2. Compare the routes shown for that combination.
  3. Open a route and inspect its individual steps and provider labels.

For instance, a route might show a bridge step followed by a swap step. The name on the bridge step tells you which provider handles the cross-chain leg; a separate provider on the swap step handles that conversion. The route’s provider names are more useful than assuming one provider serves every LI.FI transfer.

Before choosing, compare the routes available for your exact chains and tokens, then check the selected route’s steps to identify each provider.
