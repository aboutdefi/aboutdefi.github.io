Block space in blockchain: How Ethereum prices each block

Block space in blockchain is the room each block has for transactions. On Ethereum, gas prices that capacity, while MEV makes transaction order valuable.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 24, 2026 · 02:11 UTC3 min read cf1d26

Block space is the limited capacity a blockchain allocates to transactions in each block.

Ethereum measures block space in gas

On Ethereum, each operation in a transaction consumes a set amount of gas, the unit used to measure the work it requires. A simple ETH transfer uses 21,000 gas; a decentralised exchange (DEX) swap uses much more of the same block’s capacity.

Think of block space as seats on a train and gas as the space each passenger takes. The block’s gas limit caps how much transaction work fits, and validators adjust that limit by signalling. The gas limit is a capacity measure; gas is the unit used to count how much of it a transaction consumes.

Under EIP-1559, the base fee per gas moves with demand: it rises when blocks use more than the gas target, which is half the limit, and falls when they use less. The base fee is burned. Ethereum’s EIP-1559 specification sets out that fee mechanism, while its gas and fees explanation describes how gas use and the target relate.

Say demand surges and blocks keep filling beyond the target. The base fee rises block by block until demand eases; users also set a priority fee to encourage inclusion. The fee is the price of access, while block space is the scarce capacity being priced.

That capacity is not equally valuable at every position. After a token’s price moves on one exchange, the first transaction in the next block that trades against the stale price can capture an arbitrage; the same swap later in the block may find the price already corrected. This is maximal extractable value (MEV): value available through transaction selection and ordering. Flashbots adds useful background on how MEV-Boost lets validators offer block space to builders, who assemble transactions into blocks and compete to provide valuable ordering.

Block space is capacity; MEV can make position valuable

Gas fees price the amount of work a transaction uses, but MEV can make its position worth more than its gas alone suggests. That is why builder markets can price a transaction’s place in a block as well as its capacity: demand sets the fee pressure, and ordering can decide who captures an opportunity.

Related Ethereum questions

How is the Ethereum gas limit decided?

Ethereum validators adjust the gas limit by signalling, which changes the maximum transaction work each block can include. The gas target used by EIP-1559 is half that limit.

What is blob space on Ethereum?

Blob space is capacity for temporary data blobs attached to Ethereum blocks, used mainly to carry rollup data. It has a separate fee market from execution gas, so blob demand does not use the same block capacity as ordinary transactions.
