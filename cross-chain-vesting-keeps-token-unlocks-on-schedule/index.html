Cross-Chain Vesting Keeps Token Unlocks on Schedule

Cross-chain vesting applies a token release schedule across blockchains, using contracts and messages to control when beneficiaries can claim their allocations.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 17, 2026 · 00:01 UTC3 min read 70fd52

Cross-chain vesting applies a token release schedule across more than one blockchain, so beneficiaries can claim an allocation on a destination chain as it unlocks. The schedule sets how much becomes available over time; contracts and cross-chain messages coordinate where tokens can be claimed.

How does cross-chain vesting work?

A vesting contract records the allocation, beneficiary and schedule, then releases only the amount unlocked at each point. OpenZeppelin’s VestingWallet documentation describes this core model: the contract holds tokens and makes them available according to a schedule.

Cross-chain systems add a bridge or messaging layer. One design keeps the locked tokens on the source chain and sends an authenticated message when a claim becomes available; another releases tokens on the source chain before transferring them to the destination. A closer look at Paraswap and crypto transfers gives more context on the transfer layer, which is separate from the vesting schedule itself.

What does the bridge add?

The bridge carries either tokens or instructions between networks. The destination contract must verify that a release message came from an authorized source, and the system must prevent the same unlocked amount from being claimed twice.

  • Source-chain custody: Tokens stay locked at the origin until a claim or transfer is authorized.
  • Destination-chain claims: A local contract pays beneficiaries after it verifies the source-chain release.
  • Wrapped or mirrored tokens: A bridge may represent source-chain assets on another network, with redemption depending on the bridge’s backing and controls.
  • Shared schedule data: Contracts need a consistent view of the start time, cliff, unlock rate and amounts already claimed.

What are the main trade-offs?

Keeping tokens locked on the source chain makes the allocation easier to track in one place, but beneficiaries may need to bridge tokens after release. Destination-chain claims can reduce that extra step, but rely on secure message verification and accurate claim accounting across networks.

In either design, a bridge outage or rejected message can delay a claim even when the vesting schedule says tokens are unlocked. A schedule that lives on one chain is simpler to audit; a cross-chain schedule offers more flexibility but adds another contract and trust boundary.

What should a beneficiary check?

Check which chain holds the locked tokens, where claims are paid, and whether the contract’s published schedule matches the grant terms. Confirm how the system handles failed messages and whether claimed amounts are recorded to prevent duplicate withdrawals.

Cross-chain vesting is the combination of a time-based release rule and a cross-chain transfer or messaging system. The schedule determines when tokens become claimable; the bridge determines how that authorization or value reaches the other chain.
