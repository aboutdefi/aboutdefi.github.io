SyncSwap pool types shape trades and liquidity risk

SyncSwap’s Classic, Stable and Aqua pools use different pricing curves, so pool choice affects swap slippage, liquidity exposure and how deposits are managed.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 21, 2026 · 20:32 UTC3 min read ad426a

SyncSwap offers Classic, Stable and Aqua pools, whose pricing models affect how trades move prices and how liquidity providers’ deposits are exposed. The protocol’s interface lets traders compare pool quotes; the syncswap name is also used across its pool and trading screens.

What are the SyncSwap pool types?

Each pool type uses a different pricing model, according to SyncSwap’s technical documentation. Classic pools use the constant product formula x*y=k for general token pairs, while Stable pools use a hybrid curve designed for assets that trade near the same value. Aqua pools use a dynamic hybrid curve with an auto-rebalancing mechanism, and SyncSwap describes them as suited to volatile and liquid staking token pairs.

That distinction matters because a curve shapes how much the pool’s quoted price moves as a trade changes its token balances. Stable pools can keep trades between similarly priced assets closer to the peg while reserves allow; a large trade or a lost peg can still move the price. Classic pools handle pairs whose values can diverge, while Aqua adds dynamic fees that can vary with pool conditions.

How do you trade through a SyncSwap pool?

Choose the network and the two tokens, enter the amount, then review the quoted output, fee and price impact before signing. The router finds a route through available pools, so a trade may use more than one pool; the pool’s reserves and the trade size help determine the output. The interface displays the quote before confirmation, giving traders a chance to reduce the amount or cancel if the result is worse than expected.

How do you add liquidity, and which pool fits?

Open the pool section, select an existing pair and pool type, enter the deposit amounts, then review and approve the token transfers before confirming. A liquidity deposit adds assets to the pool in exchange for a share of it; the interface shows the amount and pool share before submission. Check the displayed pool and token names carefully, since different pool types can exist for the same pair.

  • Classic: a general choice for pairs whose prices move independently.
  • Stable: for assets expected to stay close in value, such as tokens tracking the same currency.
  • Aqua: for pairs the protocol supports with its dynamic pricing and fee model.

Liquidity providers earn a share of trading fees, but the value and mix of their tokens can change as traders use the pool. That exposure is often called impermanent loss: withdrawing may return a different token balance and value than simply holding the original assets. For most users, the better starting point is the pool whose model matches the pair, then comparing its available liquidity, fee and expected price impact; the pool’s current terms and quotes determine the trade-off.
