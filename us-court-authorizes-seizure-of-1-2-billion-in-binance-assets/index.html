US court authorizes seizure of $1.2 billion in Binance assets

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance assets, part of $2.7 billion frozen since October's theft.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 21, 2026 · 12:16 UTC2 min read 0f23db

The Justice Department said on Nov. 12 it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in October. The department’s announcement said the assets are part of a broader effort to interdict funds taken in the breach.

The Associated Press report said the FBI identified the theft as the work of TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group. The Justice Department said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets; AP put the loss at about $6.3 billion.

  • More than $2.7 billion frozen: U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen this amount of the stolen assets, according to the Justice Department.
  • More than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint: This is a portion of the frozen total, and the department said the court had authorized action on it.
  • More than $6 billion stolen: The Justice Department’s estimate of the Oct. 29 breach; AP reported about $6.3 billion.

The distinction matters: court authorization to seize or restrain assets is not the same as a final forfeiture. AP reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions tied to addresses used in that laundering.

The FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating recovery work with the Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, according to the department. Authorities said the next steps are continued tracing and efforts to seize and forfeit the remaining assets.
