Hong Kong bill targets four crypto services for SFC oversight

Hong Kong gazetted a bill on Nov. 20 to require virtual-asset dealers, advisers, managers and custodians to register or license with the SFC if enacted.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 22, 2026 · 16:40 UTC2 min read eb19e6

Hong Kong gazetted a bill on Nov. 20 proposing Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) licensing or registration for businesses dealing in, advising on, managing or holding virtual assets. The Hong Kong government’s announcement says the proposal would amend the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance.

Under the bill, providers carrying on those four services in Hong Kong would need an SFC licence or registration and would face a fit-and-proper test and other regulatory requirements. Banks and stored value facility licensees regulated by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority would also need SFC registration if they want to provide any virtual-asset services in the city, the government said.

The proposal extends the licensing framework beyond trading platforms. CoinDesk reported that the bill would bring crypto brokers, investment advisers, asset managers and custodians under the SFC; the government describes the covered activities as dealing, advisory, management and custody services.

The government said the framework would align with standards for conventional financial services in Hong Kong. SFC Chief Executive Julia Leung said the proposal would strengthen investor protection and provide clearer paths for digital-asset businesses, according to the government announcement.

The bill has not yet become law. It is scheduled for its first reading in the Legislative Council on Dec. 2, the government said; the announcement does not specify when the proposed requirements would take effect.
