WBTC vs LBTC: what changes when bridging to Sui?

WBTC vs LBTC when bridging to Sui: WBTC is custodian-backed; LBTC represents Babylon-staked BTC, so the choice is reserve exposure versus staking exposure.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 19, 2026 · 14:06 UTC2 min read 908720

WBTC and LBTC use the same lock-and-mint path when bridged from Ethereum to Sui, but their backing differs: WBTC represents BTC held by a custodian, while LBTC represents BTC staked through Babylon. Suibridge moves either token to Sui, where the matching bridged coin is minted to your Sui address.

Both tokens start as ERC-20 assets on Ethereum. Before bridging, you need ETH for gas and must approve the token for the bridge contract; after the Ethereum tokens are locked, the corresponding amount is minted on Sui.

Both tokens lock on Ethereum and mint on Sui

Say you bridge 0.1 WBTC. Suibridge locks 0.1 WBTC on Ethereum and mints 0.1 bridged WBTC to your Sui address. LBTC follows the same path: 0.1 LBTC is locked on Ethereum, and 0.1 bridged LBTC is minted on Sui.

The matching amounts describe the bridge transfer, not a guarantee that either token always trades at exactly the same price as BTC. The tokens have different backing arrangements, and the Sui versions are issued through a bridge.

Backing and coin type shape the choice on Sui

WBTC is an ERC-20 backed 1:1 by BTC held in reserve by a custodian; BitGo was its original custodian. That makes WBTC’s key exposure custody and reserve risk.

Lombard issues LBTC to represent BTC staked through the Babylon protocol. LBTC adds issuer and staking-protocol risk, while giving holders exposure to BTC staking. That exposure is a different proposition from holding custodian-backed WBTC.

On Sui, a ticker alone may not identify the asset a protocol accepts. The same ticker can refer to multiple Sui coin types, with a separate type for each issuing bridge. A lending or trading protocol may support one coin type and not another, even when both display as WBTC or LBTC.

Before depositing, compare the full coin type shown by the receiving Sui protocol with the coin type of the asset in your wallet. Choose the bridged WBTC if you want BTC represented through custodian reserves; choose bridged LBTC if you want the staking exposure and accept its additional protocol and issuer risks. Then confirm that the Sui app accepts that exact coin type.
