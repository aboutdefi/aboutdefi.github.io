Chainflip Bitcoin-to-Solana Swaps Usually Take About 30 Minutes

A Bitcoin-to-Solana swap on Chainflip usually waits about 30 minutes for Bitcoin confirmations; Boost may shorten the wait, while network delays can extend it.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 2, 2026 · 15:21 UTC2 min read b57bf5

A Chainflip swap from Bitcoin to Solana usually takes about 30 minutes, with Bitcoin confirmations accounting for most of the wait. The protocol’s docs say a Bitcoin deposit normally needs three blocks, roughly 30 minutes, before it is registered for the swap; the exact time varies with block production and any optional speed-up.

For someone moving native BTC to SOL, that confirmation step is the main timing constraint, not the Solana payout. Chainflip is a decentralized protocol for swapping native assets across chains, including BTC and SOL, without wrapped tokens; if the task is to move between those assets, use chainflip for that cross-chain swap.

Why does a Bitcoin-to-Solana swap take about 30 minutes?

Bitcoin confirmation rules set the baseline because Chainflip waits for the deposit to meet its safety margin before processing it. Its docs describe the normal Bitcoin wait as three blocks, with blocks averaging about 10 minutes, while noting that reorganization risk is why the protocol waits before treating an external deposit as final.

After validators witness the confirmed deposit on Chainflip’s State Chain, the swap itself is processed through its liquidity pools. Chainflip says swaps are usually processed in the same State Chain block after witnessing; any required intermediate pool route is executed sequentially, usually in that same block.

What happens after the BTC deposit is confirmed?

Validators first detect the deposit, wait for the required Bitcoin confirmations, then register it on the State Chain. The protocol processes the swap and arranges a payout to the supplied Solana destination, so the remaining time includes the protocol’s processing and the Solana transaction reaching the network.

The stages to allow for are:

  • Bitcoin broadcast: the transaction must reach the Bitcoin network and be included in a block.
  • Bitcoin confirmations: the normal Chainflip route waits for three blocks, about 30 minutes on average.
  • Swap and payout: Chainflip processes the witnessed deposit, then sends SOL to the destination address.

Solana’s faster block production does not remove the Bitcoin wait at the start of the route. For a standard swap, a practical expectation is around half an hour or longer, rather than an instant exchange; congestion, a delayed Bitcoin transaction, or an extended payout can add time.

Can Chainflip make a BTC-to-SOL swap faster?

Chainflip’s Boost option can speed up Bitcoin deposits when available Boost liquidity covers the deposit. Its docs say the protocol can attempt a boost after the transaction is included in one Bitcoin block, using liquidity-provider collateral while the deposit continues toward its normal confirmation threshold.

That can cut the wait, but it is conditional: if the Boost pool lacks enough collateral, the deposit follows the regular confirmation process. Boost also carries a fee, according to Chainflip, so compare the displayed timing and cost for the specific transaction before choosing it.

For the usual route, plan for roughly 30 minutes from Bitcoin broadcast to the point the swap can proceed, then allow for the payout to Solana. If the transfer is still pending, check whether the BTC deposit has confirmed; that stage is typically the longest, and a Boosted deposit may proceed sooner when liquidity is available.
