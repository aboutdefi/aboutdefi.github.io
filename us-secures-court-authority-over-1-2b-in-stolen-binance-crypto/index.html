US secures court authority over $1.2B in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says it has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, as efforts to trace funds continue.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 21, 2026 · 08:33 UTC2 min read 12816e

U.S. authorities obtained court authorization on Nov. 12 to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said, as investigators and private firms continue tracing the funds.

The Justice Department announcement says more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets has been frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts. The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of that frozen total; the department did not say all of it had been finally forfeited or returned.

The FBI attributes the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, a name it uses for North Korean military cyber actors. The department said the theft occurred on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets.

Tracing and recovery efforts continue

For Binance users and other crypto holders, the distinction between frozen and forfeited funds matters: freezing can prevent assets from moving, while forfeiture requires a legal process to determine their disposition. The Associated Press reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then assesses competing claims to recovered funds.

The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors are still laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. U.S. agencies are working with virtual asset service providers to trace and interdict those transfers, and the FBI has urged providers to block transactions tied to addresses associated with the stolen assets.

The department also said the FBI, U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating recovery work. Efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets remain ongoing, according to the Justice Department.
