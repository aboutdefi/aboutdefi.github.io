How to Use Bitcoin as Collateral Through a Relay Bridge

A relay bridge can make Bitcoin usable as collateral on another chain, but users must check custody, asset matching, confirmations and liquidation rules first.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 16, 2026 · 17:52 UTC2 min read fe0efa

A relay bridge can let a user represent Bitcoin on another blockchain and post that asset as collateral, if the bridge and lending protocol support it. The exact route depends on how the bridge handles BTC: some systems rely on custodians, while others use proofs or other mechanisms to authorize transfers.

Before sending funds, check the bridge’s supported Bitcoin asset, destination network and lending market. If a transfer is taking longer than expected, see relay bridge pending times for details on what can keep it waiting.

How does Bitcoin become collateral on another chain?

The bridge creates or releases a token on the destination chain that is intended to correspond to Bitcoin held or verified elsewhere. The lending protocol accepts that token as collateral; it does not usually accept native BTC directly because Bitcoin and the destination chain do not share the same transaction system.

That distinction matters: a token’s BTC backing, redemption process and transfer controls depend on its issuer or bridge design. A displayed BTC price alone does not establish that the token is redeemable for Bitcoin.

What steps should you follow to post Bitcoin collateral?

Use the bridge and lending market’s instructions for the specific asset and network. A typical process is:

  • Confirm the token’s exact name and contract address in the lending market.
  • Check the bridge’s supported route, fees, minimums and required confirmations.
  • Send a small test amount if the route is unfamiliar, then bridge the remaining BTC.
  • Approve the destination token in the lending protocol and supply it as collateral.

After supplying it, verify the position appears in the protocol and review its collateral value, borrowing limit and liquidation threshold. Keep enough room below the borrowing limit to absorb price moves, fees or a change in the token’s market price.

What risks can affect Bitcoin collateral?

There are separate risks at each step: Bitcoin transaction delays, bridge or custodian failure, a loss of confidence in the bridged token, and lending protocol liquidation. If the token trades below BTC or becomes hard to redeem, the protocol may value it differently from native Bitcoin.

For most users, borrowing conservatively—or not borrowing against a bridged asset they cannot redeem—is the simpler choice. Before committing funds, check the bridge’s redemption terms and the lending market’s liquidation rules; those determine what happens when the collateral’s value falls.
