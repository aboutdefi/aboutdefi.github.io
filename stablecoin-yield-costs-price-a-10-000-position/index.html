Stablecoin Yield Costs: Price a $10,000 Position

The total yearly cost of keeping a stablecoin yield position in DeFi is gas plus fees: calculate it by chain, balance, claims and vault terms before comparing yield.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 28, 2026 · 01:44 UTC3 min read 3e9e46

A stablecoin yield position’s yearly cost is fixed transaction gas plus fees and slippage tied to its balance, so the chain, claim count and vault terms determine whether it outweighs the yield.

Count the transactions first. Entry can require a token approval and a deposit; add each reward claim, any cooldown activation where staking applies, and the final withdrawal. A transaction fee is gas used multiplied by gas price, paid in the chain’s native token, so convert each transaction’s cost to dollars when totaling the year.

Gas stays fixed while vault fees grow with the balance

Gas costs roughly the same for a given transaction whether the position is $1,000 or $10,000. A vault’s management or performance fee may reduce yield, while swaps or peg slippage can add costs on entry and exit. Include only the fees the chosen product actually charges.

Aave’s supply APY already reflects its reserve factor, the share of borrower interest sent to the Aave treasury. Don’t subtract that factor again. Aave v3 also runs on L2 networks including Arbitrum, Base and Optimism, where lower fixed gas can make small positions less costly to maintain.

For an Aave staking comparison, choose an Aave staking yield route to see which option fits idle stablecoins and which costs to include. Lending, staking and ERC-4626 vaults can have different claim steps and fee terms, so the route changes the cost calculation.

A $10,000 example shows when fixed costs matter

Take a $10,000 USDC position for one year. Suppose its approvals, deposit, two reward claims and withdrawal total $12 in gas; a vault charges a 0.4% annual management fee on assets; and entry and exit each incur 0.1% slippage. The fee is $40 and slippage totals $20, making yearly cost $12 + $40 + $20 = $72, or 0.72% of the balance.

Use this calculation for another position: yearly cost % = (fixed gas in dollars + fee rate × balance + slippage in dollars) ÷ balance. With the example’s 0.6% proportional costs and $12 fixed gas, the two cost components are equal at $2,000: $12 divided by 0.006. Below that size, gas weighs more; above it, proportional costs weigh more.

On an L2 market, fixed gas can become negligible, leaving fees and slippage as the main cost line. That removes much of the small-position penalty: total the expected claims and exit once, then compare the resulting cost percentage with the position’s expected yield before choosing where to hold it.
