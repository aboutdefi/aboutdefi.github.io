Base sequencer fees: From Ethereum costs to revenue

How Base makes money from sequencer fees: it collects L2 execution and data fees, pays Ethereum to post transaction batches, then keeps the remainder.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 27, 2026 · 23:28 UTC2 min read b74f40

Base earns revenue from L2 execution fees and L1 data fees, then pays Ethereum to post transaction data and keeps what remains after those costs and its share to the Optimism Collective.

Base fees enter vaults before Ethereum costs

Base’s sequencer orders transactions and collects three possible charges: an L2 base fee, an optional priority fee and an L1 data fee. The fees accrue to protocol vaults, so a transaction’s total gas charge combines payment for execution on Base with the cost of publishing its data to Ethereum.

Base’s fee vaults are predeploys at 0x4200...0011 (SequencerFeeVault), 0x4200...0019 (BaseFeeVault) and 0x4200...001A (L1FeeVault). On Basescan, you can look up those addresses and follow their onchain activity to see fee income accumulating in the contracts.

Base pays Ethereum gas to post transaction batches, mainly as blobs since EIP-4844. Those posting costs vary with Ethereum’s data fees and the amount of data in a batch; the gap between user fees and those costs is Base’s margin before the Collective share.

Base bridge moves supported assets between Ethereum and Base, while the revenue calculation follows transactions that execute on Base. For cross-chain context, bridge crypto to Base helps show where an asset moves; Base still incurs Ethereum posting costs for its transaction data.

A Base swap shows where the margin goes

A swap on Base pays the L2 base fee, any priority fee and an L1 data fee. The sequencer orders it with other transactions, fees flow into the vaults, and Base later pays Ethereum to post the batch containing that swap.

What remains after the batch cost is not all Base’s net revenue. Base shares revenue with the Optimism Collective at the greater of 2.5% of sequencer revenue or 15% of net onchain profit; after that share, the remainder is Base’s net revenue.

That fee path applies to everyday Base activity, including swaps and other transactions that use the network. Each transaction adds its fees to the vault flow, and its data is grouped into a batch for posting to Ethereum.
