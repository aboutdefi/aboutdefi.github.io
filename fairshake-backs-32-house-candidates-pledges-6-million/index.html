Fairshake backs 32 House candidates, pledges $6 million

Fairshake will back 32 House candidates who supported the stalled CLARITY Act, including six receiving $1 million each, as November midterms approach.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 7, 20262 min read 342e53

Fairshake announced Monday, October 5, that it will support 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, including six who will each receive $1 million in spending, according to the Associated Press report. The slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, all of whom supported the crypto market structure bill known as the CLARITY Act.

Which candidates will receive $1 million?

Fairshake named three Democrats — Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California — for $1 million in support each. The three Republicans receiving the same amount are Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan.

The group did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates. Its spokesperson, Geoff Vetter, told the AP that Fairshake supports candidates from both parties based on their positions on crypto.

Why is Fairshake backing these lawmakers?

The candidates backed the CLARITY Act, which would establish rules for digital asset markets. The House passed the bill in July 2025, but the Senate blocked it in September after lawmakers failed to agree on ethics provisions, Roll Call reported.

Democrats sought stronger limits on President Donald Trump’s crypto interests. The stalled bill had been a priority for the industry, which says a federal framework would provide regulatory certainty.

Will Fairshake spend more before the election?

Fairshake has not said whether it plans additional spending in House or Senate races before November. The AP reported that Fairshake and its affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash at the end of August.

The group has also pledged nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. The House candidates’ support is part of Fairshake’s effort to back lawmakers who supported the bill as voters head to the November midterms.
