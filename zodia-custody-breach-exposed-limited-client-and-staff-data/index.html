Zodia Custody breach exposed limited client and staff data

Zodia Custody says an intruder accessed limited client and employee contact data after probing its platform, while its investigation found no unauthorised transactions.

The Blocktape Editors Nov 27, 2026 · 17:56 UTC2 min read e7a02c

Zodia Custody detected attempts to probe its platform on Nov. 23 and found an unauthorised party had accessed limited client information the next day, the crypto custodian said. In a statement from Zodia CEO Richard Clark, the company said some client and employee business contact names and email addresses were exposed.

Zodia said the accessed information also included publicly available organisation details. It did not say how many clients were affected, how the intruder gained access or who was responsible.

After detecting the probing, Zodia temporarily took its business-to-business API offline, added security measures and restored the service, Clark said. The API connects institutional clients’ systems to Zodia’s platform. The company said it had contacted clients and informed relevant regulatory authorities.

“Based on our investigation to date, there have been no unauthorised transactions,” Clark said. Zodia has not reported that client assets were moved. The company’s statement does not say whether the investigation is complete.

Standard Chartered owns Zodia Custody, which provides cryptocurrency custody services to institutional clients. Reuters reported that the breach came weeks after the bank increased its stake in the unit to 100% as it expanded its digital asset business. The bank’s plans for Zodia were not part of the company’s breach statement.

The company has restored the API and notified affected clients and regulators, according to Reuters’ report on Clark’s account. Zodia has not disclosed how many clients were affected or identified the unauthorised party.
