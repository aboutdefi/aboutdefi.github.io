What to Do When a Cross-Chain Transfer Gets Stuck

A stuck bridge transfer may be pending on the source chain, awaiting a destination action, or eligible for a refund; check its status before retrying.

The Blocktape Editors Oct 16, 2026 · 06:16 UTC3 min read 3777ff

If a cross-chain transfer gets stuck, check the source transaction and the bridge’s status before sending funds again. A bridge may lock or burn assets on one chain, then mint, release or swap assets on another; a confirmed source transaction does not by itself prove the destination step finished. Keep the transaction hash, source and destination networks, token and amount to hand.

How do I check where the transfer stopped?

Start with the source-chain explorer: it shows whether the transaction is pending, failed or confirmed. If it failed, the bridge call did not complete; if it confirmed, check the bridge’s own tracker or the destination-chain explorer for the next step. The destination token may have a different contract address from the source token, so verify the asset and recipient address rather than relying only on the wallet’s display.

Bridge designs and supported routes change over time. For historical context on Bitcoin routes, see RenBridge routes and what still works. The specific bridge’s status page is the right place to check an active transfer.

What should I do if the source transaction is pending?

A pending source transaction has not yet been recorded as complete by that chain. Check the wallet’s fee and nonce details; if the wallet supports it, speeding up or cancelling may be possible, but that action applies to the source transaction and does not recover a transfer already confirmed on-chain.

  • Record the transaction hash and confirm the source network.
  • Check the bridge tracker for pending, completed, expired or refunded status.
  • If the source transaction confirmed, search the destination address and correct network for the expected token.
  • Follow the bridge’s instructions if it requires a claim, finalization or refund.

When can I retry or request a refund?

Retry only after confirming the first source transaction failed or the bridge says the transfer was refunded. Across’s documentation, for example, says an unfilled deposit can become eligible for a refund after its fill deadline; that process depends on the bridge and transfer, so do not assume every pending transfer can be reclaimed the same way.

If the tracker shows a confirmed source transaction but no destination delivery or refund, contact the bridge through its official support channel with the hash and status details. Never share a seed phrase or private key, and do not sign a new transaction sent by an unsolicited “support” account. The next step is to follow the bridge’s status or recovery instructions for that specific transfer.
